About

Tracks how the precision of the living literature (the share of published surviving findings that are true) evolves over successive research generations. Each dip corresponds to a false finding entering the record; recoveries follow successful replications that prune false strands.

What this model does

Scientific knowledge is represented as a growing forest of findings. Every generation, the field performs exactly one act. With probability r it replicates an existing finding; otherwise (1 - r) it pursues novelty. Novelty either founds a brand-new independent paradigm strand (probability p_new_root) or extends an existing finding. The headline outcome is the precision of the surviving literature: the fraction of published, un-pruned findings that are actually true.

This operationalises March's exploration vs. exploitation and Kuhn's paradigm pluralism as a tunable stochastic process.

Optimal Replication Rate tab

Every simulation automatically sweeps r from 0 to 1: in steps of 0.01 at the edges (0–5% and 95–100%) and steps of 0.05 in the middle (10–90%). At each step, 100 independent simulations are run (varying only the random seed) and their final precision values are averaged. The bar at the precision-maximising rate is labelled r*. Error bars show one standard deviation across the 100 runs.

Mechanics, step by step

  1. Draw act. With probability r replicate; else pursue novelty. (If no living nodes exist yet, force novelty.)
  2. Novelty to root or extension. With probability p_new_root spawn a new root (parentId = null); its truth is drawn via p_next. Otherwise pick a parent among living nodes via the Extension Targets setting.
  3. Inheritance. If the chosen base is false, the extension is necessarily false. If the base is true, the extension is true with probability p_next. Extensions are worthless if the base is false.
  4. Testing and publication. A claim is published only if its test reads positive: probability alpha if true, beta if false.
  5. Replication and pruning. A replication re-tests a living node chosen by the Replication Targets setting. A failed (negative) re-test is suppressed with probability f; otherwise it is published and the refuted node plus all its descendants are recursively pruned (a retraction cascade). Pruned nodes stay visible in the tree, drawn in grey.

Reading the phylogeny

Each dot is a finding; edges link a finding to the parent it extends. Roots (new paradigms) are scattered across the canvas, ringed, and each lineage grows outward in its own random direction, so independent paradigms occupy their own regions. Colour encodes fate: green true positive, red false positive, and grey pruned (refuted by a published replication, along with everything that depended on it).

Why an interior optimum?

At r = 0 nothing is ever checked, so false positives accumulate and precision collapses. At r = 1 the field only re-checks and never grows, starving the literature. Somewhere in between, replication prunes enough false strands to keep precision high while still permitting growth, the optimal replication rate.
